    Preface Learning Outcomes

    The following learning outcomes describe the knowledge and skills students are expected to develop in MATH 007B. Textbook section references refer to Calculus for Biology and Medicine by Neuhauser and Roper, 4th edition (2018), unless otherwise indicated.
    🔗

    Antiderivatives.

    (Chapter 1) Textbook: Neuhauser and Roper, Section 5.10.
    🔗
    1. State the definition of an antiderivative.
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Find general antiderivatives of power, exponential, and trigonometric functions.
      🔗
      🔗
    3. Find general antiderivatives of simple composite functions.
      🔗
      🔗
    4. Solve initial value problems involving antiderivatives.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗

    The Definite Integral.

    (Chapter 2) Textbook: Neuhauser and Roper, Section 6.1.
    🔗
    1. Interpret definite integrals geometrically as signed area.
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Approximate definite integrals using Riemann sums with left endpoints.
      🔗
      🔗
    3. Evaluate definite integrals using geometric reasoning.
      🔗
      🔗
    4. Explain the definition of the definite integral as a limit of Riemann sums.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗

    The Fundamental Theorem of Calculus.

    (Chapter 3) Textbook: Neuhauser and Roper, Section 6.2.
    🔗
    1. State and apply Part 1 of the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus.
      🔗
      🔗
    2. State and apply Part 2 of the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗

    Cumulative Change.

    (Section 4.2) Textbook: Neuhauser and Roper, Section 6.3.1.
    🔗
    1. Use definite integrals to calculate cumulative change over an interval.
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Interpret cumulative change in applications.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗

    Average Values.

    (Section 4.3) Textbook: Neuhauser and Roper, Section 6.3.2.
    🔗
    1. State and explain the definition of the average value of a function over an interval.
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Calculate and interpret the average value of a function over an interval.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗

    The Mean Value Theorem.

    (Section 4.4) Textbook: Neuhauser and Roper, Section 6.3.3.
    🔗
    1. State, illustrate, and apply the Mean Value Theorem for integrals.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗

    Areas.

    (Section 4.1) Textbook: Neuhauser and Roper, Section 6.3.4.
    🔗
    1. Calculate the area between two curves over a specified interval.
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Determine appropriate limits of integration from the intersection points of two curves.
      🔗
      🔗
    3. Calculate area when the upper and lower curves change over the interval.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗

    Volumes of Solids.

    (Section 4.5) Textbook: Neuhauser and Roper, Section 6.3.5.
    🔗
    1. Apply the Disk Method to calculate volumes of solids of revolution about the \(x\)- or \(y\)-axis.
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Apply the Washer Method to calculate volumes of solids of revolution about the \(x\)- or \(y\)-axis.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗

    Arc Length.

    (Section 4.6) Textbook: Not covered in Neuhauser and Roper.
    🔗
    1. Set up and evaluate integrals for the arc length of a curve.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗

    The Substitution Rule.

    (Section 5.1) Textbook: Neuhauser and Roper, Section 7.1.
    🔗
    1. Apply \(u\)-substitution to evaluate indefinite and definite integrals.
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Recognize integrals for which \(u\)-substitution is an appropriate method.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗

    Integration by Parts and Practicing Integration.

    (Section 5.3) Textbook: Neuhauser and Roper, Section 7.2.
    🔗
    1. Apply Integration by Parts to evaluate integrals.
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Apply Integration by Parts repeatedly when necessary.
      🔗
      🔗
    3. Combine \(u\)-substitution and Integration by Parts when appropriate.
      🔗
      🔗
    4. Recognize whether \(u\)-substitution or Integration by Parts is an appropriate integration method.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗

    Rational Functions and Partial Fractions.

    (Section 5.4) Textbook: Neuhauser and Roper, Section 7.3.
    🔗
    1. Use Partial Fraction Decomposition to integrate rational functions whose denominators contain distinct linear factors.
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Use Partial Fraction Decomposition when the denominator contains repeated linear factors.
      🔗
      🔗
    3. Use Partial Fraction Decomposition when the denominator contains irreducible quadratic factors.
      🔗
      🔗
    4. Use polynomial long division before Partial Fraction Decomposition when appropriate.
      🔗
      🔗
    5. Recognize rational-function integrals that can be evaluated without Partial Fraction Decomposition.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗

    Improper Integrals.

    (Section 5.5) Textbook: Neuhauser and Roper, Section 7.4.
    🔗
    1. Recognize and evaluate improper integrals with infinite discontinuities.
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Recognize and evaluate improper integrals over infinite intervals.
      🔗
      🔗
    3. Determine whether an improper integral converges or diverges.
      🔗
      🔗
    4. Apply the Direct Comparison Test to determine convergence or divergence.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗

    Trigonometric Integrals.

    (Chapter 6) Textbook: APEX Calculus, Section 6.3.
    🔗
    1. Evaluate integrals involving powers of \(\sin x\) and \(\cos x\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Evaluate integrals involving powers of \(\tan x\) and \(\sec x\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    3. Use trigonometric identities to rewrite integrands in a form that can be integrated.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗

    Trigonometric Substitution.

    (Section 6.3) Textbook: APEX Calculus, Section 6.4.
    🔗
    1. Apply Trigonometric Substitution to integrals involving expressions of the form \(a^2-x^2\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Apply Trigonometric Substitution to integrals involving expressions of the form \(x^2+a^2\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    3. Apply Trigonometric Substitution to integrals involving expressions of the form \(x^2-a^2\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    4. Recognize when an integral involving one of these expressions can be evaluated without Trigonometric Substitution.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗

    Solving Separable Differential Equations.

    (Section 7.4) Textbook: Neuhauser and Roper, Section 8.1.
    🔗
    1. Recognize separable differential equations.
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Find general and particular solutions to separable differential equations.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗

    Equilibria and Their Stability.

    (Section 7.5) Textbook: Neuhauser and Roper, Section 8.2.
    🔗
    1. Determine the equilibria of an autonomous differential equation algebraically and graphically.
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Construct and interpret a phase line for an autonomous differential equation.
      🔗
      🔗
    3. Classify equilibria as stable or unstable from the direction of the vector field.
      🔗
      🔗
    4. Determine the long-term behavior of solutions from a phase line.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗

    Differential Equation Models.

    (Section 7.8) Textbook: Neuhauser and Roper, Section 8.3.
    🔗
    1. Formulate and analyze differential equation models arising from applications.
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Interpret model parameters, solutions, equilibria, and long-term behavior in context.
      🔗
      🔗
